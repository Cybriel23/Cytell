### 1.2 NGINX2Root reaches PAN-OS and Panorama: CVE-2026-123633 / CVE-2026-123007, some hotfixes due 23 Nov (NEW for PAN-OS, follow-up for NGINX)
Palo Alto Networks has a portfolio advisory for the two critical NGINX HTTP/2 flaws (vendor CVSS 4.0 **9.2**). **CVE-2026-123007** is a flow-control integer underflow that leads to a heap overflow. **CVE-2026-123633** is a use-after-free in proxying when worker connection slots run low. Both are unauthenticated. PAN-OS and Panorama are exposed **only when HTTP/2 is enabled**.

Affected and fixed versions:

| Branch | Affected | Fixed |
|---|---|---|
| 12.2 | before **12.2.4-h3** | 12.2.4-h3 |
| 12.1 | 12.1.5 – 12.1.7-h14 and 12.1.8 – 12.1.10-h5 | **12.1.10-h6**, or **12.1.7-h15** (ETA 23 Nov) |
| 11.2 | 11.2.8 – 11.2.16-h2 | **11.2.16-h3**, or **11.2.10-h19 / 11.2.13-h6** (ETA 23 Nov) |

**12.1.2–12.1.4** and **11.2.0–11.2.7** need no action. Palo Alto reports no malicious exploitation and is patching its SaaS products itself.

On the NGINX side, F5 fixed all five NGINX2Root bugs on **18 Nov** in **1.31.9 (mainline) and 1.30.7 (stable)** plus NGINX Plus. Rapid7 has published a PoC for CVE-2026-123007 that reaches a worker shell. It is slow and noisy: it sends a huge volume of HTTP/2 control frames over minutes. Fake "exploits" are also circulating on GitHub. There are no in-the-wild reports yet, and neither CVE is in KEV. Because NGINX is embedded in many appliances, more vendor portfolio advisories are likely.
**Action:** On PAN-OS and Panorama, upgrade where a fixed build exists. Otherwise disable the HTTP/2 option until the 23 Nov hotfixes arrive. On standalone NGINX, upgrade, or set `http2 off` as a stopgap. That stopgap does not cover the separate HTTP/3 bug CVE-2026-121810. Inventory appliances that embed NGINX, and alert on repeated worker crashes (`worker process ... exited on signal`) and on long-lived HTTP/2 connections with abnormal control-frame rates.
**Sources:** [Palo Alto Networks advisory](https://security.paloaltonetworks.com/CVE-2026-123633), [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/19/nginx2root-nginx-vulnerabilities/), [Wiz](https://wiz.io/blog/nginx-http2-rce-vulnerabilities), [Tenable FAQ](https://www.tenable.com/blog/nginx2root-cve-2026-123007-frequently-asked-questions-about-five-critical-nginx)

### 1.3 Microsoft Entra Connect Health API flaw exploited to read tenant data (NEW)
Microsoft is notifying tenants that an API bug in the Entra Connect Health cloud service was exploited. Microsoft introduced the bug on **23 Jul**, detected it on **9 Nov** and fixed it server-side on **19 Nov**. Its investigation, concluded on **20 Nov**, found unauthorized **read-only** access to:
- **Administrator and technical-contact email addresses**
- Tenant ID, display name, initial domain, country and region
- Entra license tier, directory-sync status and last-sync time
- An **expired** agent-registration certificate

The bug could also have allowed changes to monitoring configuration, but Microsoft found none. Microsoft says no passwords, hashes, tokens, valid keys, AD topology or subscription data were exposed. It has not said how many tenants are affected or who the actor was. MSPs report notifications for several customer tenants. The data gives attackers a ready-made target list for admin-focused phishing and help-desk social engineering.
**Action:** Check Azure Service Health for the advisory on your tenant. Warn named admins and contacts about targeted phishing. Enforce phishing-resistant MFA and PIM for privileged roles. Review Entra audit and sign-in logs and Azure Activity Logs since July for unusual admin activity and Connect Health configuration changes. Microsoft's read-only GetTenant script (aka.ms/ConnectHealth/scripts) shows what the API returns. It shows current values only, not what the attackers saw.
**Sources:** [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/microsoft-entra-connect-health-flaw-exploited-to-steal-tenant-data/), [Cyber Security News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/microsoft-entra-connect-health-flaw/)

### 1.4 Rackspace Spot breach: leaked credentials gave access to customer Kubernetes Secrets (NEW)
Rackspace told customers that on **17 Nov (about 00:20–22:00 UTC)** an unauthorized party used **leaked credentials** to act as legitimate users in some Rackspace Spot organizations. During that window the attacker could:
- create pods in customer clusters
- **read Kubernetes Secrets**
- list nodes and volumes
- obtain the user's Spot API and refresh tokens

Rackspace learned of the intrusion from a customer report on 18 Nov. It revoked tokens, removed the unauthorized pods and rotated keys. The admin console was down for days while Rackspace and Platform9 cleaned the environment, and service was restored on **21 Nov**. Rackspace has not disclosed how many customers were affected or whether data was stolen.

IOCs: IP **23.224.79[.]106**, range **23.224.0.0/15**, and pod/object name prefixes **`i1`**, **`glm-`** and **`probe`**.
**Action:** If you use Spot:
1. Reissue Spot API tokens, replace refresh tokens in automation and download fresh kubeconfigs.
2. **Rotate every credential stored in cluster Secrets.**
3. From 17 Nov onward, review logs for the services those Secrets unlock (databases, registries, Git, object storage, SaaS) and look for the IOC range and pod prefixes.

**Sources:** [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/rackspace-spot-breach-let-hackers-access-customer-kubernetes-secrets/)

### 1.5 F5 BIG-IP APM / iRules hotfixes: still no reported exploitation, configuration details clarified (follow-up, ONGOING)
F5's per-CVE article for **CVE-2026-123637** (CVSS v3.1 **9.8**, v4.0 **9.3**) shows it is a data-plane stack overflow. It is reachable only when APM is provisioned and a virtual server has a per-request policy that includes both the **Client Information** and **Endpoint Security** agents. Restricting the management interface therefore does not help.

Affected: **21.1.0, 17.5.0–17.5.1 and 17.1.0–17.1.3**. The engineering hotfixes are mapped per installed build in K000164239, for example **BIGIP-21.1.0.4.0.19.3-ENG** and **BIGIP-17.1.3.7.0.21.4-ENG**. The two 17.x builds listed for each base release are **not interchangeable**. Customers already running a custom engineering hotfix need a replacement build from F5 Support. F5 lists no workaround. No source reports exploitation of the 18 Nov batch yet, but F5 APM has a recent zero-day record (CVE-2026-94127).
**Action:** Match your full installed build string to K000164239 before installing. Prioritize virtual servers with the affected per-request-policy configuration that are reachable from untrusted networks. Watch for TMM crashes and restarts.
**Sources:** [GBHackers](https://gbhackers.com/critical-f5-big-ip-stack-buffer-overflow-flaw/), [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/f5-releases-emergency-patches-for-critical-big-ip-rce-flaws/)

## 4. Threat Watch
- **Webworm's custom SoftEther bridge (Rapid7):** Rapid7 analysed a rebranded **SoftEther VPN Bridge 4.44 build 9807**, attributed with moderate confidence to the China-aligned espionage group **Webworm** (which targets European governments). The file is named `netcapa.exe`, poses as "Network Monitor Software" and uses the label "**NtMonitor VPN**". It contains no malware as such. Instead, it bridges attacker networks into victim LANs. Changes from stock SoftEther:
  - a changed handshake watermark, so stock peers cannot connect
  - decoy HTTP paths such as `/update/alive.cgi`
  - an embedded `hamcore.se2`
  - a `setting.conf` file encrypted with an RC4-like cipher, whose `RCEN` marker carries the key, so it can be decrypted offline
  - leftovers that give it away: the `SEVPNBRIDGE` service name and a `vpnbridge.pdb` path

  C2 sits in **AS20473 (Vultr)**, and only one VirusTotal engine flagged the sample. Hunt for `POST /update/picture.cgi` requests that declare `image/jpeg` but carry a PNG body. Rapid7 published YARA rules and IOCs. [Rapid7](https://www.rapid7.com/blog/post/tr-spying-on-governments-no-malware-custom-webworms-toolkit-softether-bridge/), [GBHackers](https://gbhackers.com/china-linked-webworm-softether-vpn/)
- **BADBOX 3.0 (HUMAN Satori):** the Android/AOSP supply-chain botnet has rebuilt after the 2025 disruptions. At its peak it generated **about 40 billion ad bid requests a day**, over **4%** of the CTV traffic HUMAN observed and more than three times the previous record. It uses hidden-window video ads, fraudulent SSAI requests, and enrolment in 11 residential proxy networks. One of them, **PXYEDGE**, is a rebrand of IpMoYu with about 200k advertised nodes. HUMAN links it to a Chinese electronics maker via a factory-installed launcher. Google's takedown of the XCPE operation hit some of its C2. Block unmanaged Android TV boxes from corporate networks and watch for residential-proxy egress. [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/badbox-30-botnet-returns-with-record-breaking-40-billion-daily-ad-requests/)
- **Bazuka Android banking trojan (ThreatFabric):** a modular framework (dropper plus core plus **23 modules**) loaded in memory via `InMemoryDexClassLoader`. It uses two ChaCha20-Poly1305 WebSocket channels and abuses Accessibility and ADB. Capabilities include keylogging, SMS and OTP interception, lock-screen PIN capture, overlays built from captured screen images, and NFC card relay. The **operator panel uses LLMs to score which victims are worth pursuing**. Lures include a fake "TikTok18+" app. The builder settings suggest Google Play distribution was planned, but none was seen. [GBHackers](https://gbhackers.com/ai-powered-bazuka-banking-trojan-android-users/)
- **DPRK IT-worker front poses as a contractor (Silent Push):** "The Great House" (`thegreathouse[.]us`, a fake Lynchburg, Virginia construction and engineering firm) was found by pivoting on the registrant `devtop0109[@]gmail[.]com`, which appears on a list of about 1,400 known DPRK IT-worker emails. On video calls, its operator "Edward" showed signs of a live face-swap and refused a hand-occlusion test. It is a vendor-side twist on Contagious Interview. No contracts or payments were found. Verify contractor licences with state boards, and use occlusion and profile checks on video calls. [GBHackers](https://gbhackers.com/north-korean-fake-construction-company-ai-deepfakes/)
