# Daily Cyber Threat Intelligence Briefing
Monday, 23 November 2026 · Coverage: 20 Nov 2026 06:00 UTC – 23 Nov 2026 06:00 UTC (weekend) and follow-ups on tracked topics

## Executive Summary
The weekend was quiet for new disclosures, but several deadlines fall today. Four concerns lead. First, four CISA KEV deadlines fall **today, Monday 23 Nov**: **vCenter CVE-2026-123890**, **Cisco Expressway CVE-2026-76466**, **Apache Roller CVE-2026-129448** and **SAP NetWeaver CVE-2025-42884**. Four more are due **tomorrow, Tuesday 24 Nov**: Office, Chromium, BroadWorks and Openwave. Google's ARMORY CASTLE write-up now shows the vCenter flaw was exploited from **early September**, and the actor's BONESAW tool was already in use in **July**. Any vCenter exposed before 19 Nov should be treated as possibly compromised, not just unpatched. Second, **GL.iNet** confirmed attackers abused a flaw in its **GoodCloud** remote-management service to plant malware on routers and **KVM devices**. Fixing the cloud flaw does not clean devices that are already infected, and KVMs reach the computers attached to them. Third, the **PAN-OS NGINX2Root hotfixes** for 12.1.7 and 11.2.10/11.2.13 are due today. Until they are installed, disable HTTP/2. Fourth, **Cleverbridge**, a reseller platform used by many software vendors, confirmed theft of **card numbers, expiry dates and CVVs**. Watch this week for: the KEV deadlines on 23–24 Nov, the PAN-OS hotfix releases, GL.iNet's stable firmware and any victim count, The Gentlemen's NVIDIA countdown (28 Nov), Tomcat 11.0.30, any FBI statement on NCIC, and Thanksgiving/Black Friday fraud and outage risk.

## 1. Risk Watch

### 1.1 KEV deadlines today; vCenter CVE-2026-123890 exploited since September (follow-up, ONGOING)
The CISA catalog has not changed since **20 Nov** (version 2026.11.20, **1,790** entries).

Due **today, 23 Nov**:
- **CVE-2026-123890**, VMware vCenter `vmdird` SRP-6a session-key disclosure (CVSS **9.8**)
- **CVE-2026-76466**, Cisco Expressway clustered root RCE, fixed in **X15.5.20**
- **CVE-2026-129448**, Apache Roller deserialization
- **CVE-2025-42884**, SAP NetWeaver JNDI injection

Due **24 Nov**:
- **CVE-2026-124194**, Microsoft Office/Outlook
- **CVE-2026-125350**, Google Chromium
- **CVE-2026-20320**, Cisco BroadWorks
- **CVE-2026-111793**, Openwave Webtop

New this weekend: Google Threat Intelligence and Mandiant (GTIG) say **ARMORY CASTLE (ex-UNC5221)** used the vCenter flaw from **early September**, through a Go tool called **BONESAW**. BONESAW steals vmdird's `data.mdb` (LMDB), hijacks the SAML Security Token Service (STS) and creates an SSO administrator account. Google also found BONESAW in **July** on a server already patched against an earlier vCenter authentication bypass (**CVE-2026-59309**). The actor got in through BeyondTrust Remote Support (**CVE-2026-102764**) and then moved across Linux appliances to vCenter. Its other tools are:
- **SLIMSAVAGE** and **CAGEMATCH**, webshells
- **BEARBONES**, a backdoor
- **SAWSNAKE**, a SAML-forging utility

After that it added SSH keys to root's `authorized_keys` and inventoried ESXi datastores. The vCenter fixes are **9.1.1.0200, 9.0.2.0200 and 8.0 U3m**. **7.0 gets no fix**.

**Action:** Finish vCenter and Expressway patching today, and isolate or retire vCenter 7.0. Because exploitation has gone on since September, patching alone does not close the incident. For vCenter:
- Hunt for unexpected `*@vsphere.local` administrators and new SSH keys on vCenter and ESXi root.
- Hunt for the 20–21 Nov artifacts: `/tmp/123`, `/usr/bin/vmtoolscs`.
- Look for unusual SAML token issuance.
- On any hit, **rotate the vCenter STS signing certificate and all SSO credentials**.

For Expressway, check `erlang.log` for `wombat@127.0.0.1` and look for traffic to **66.179.31[.]32** or **18.130.47[.]116**. Confirm Office and Chromium-family builds (Chrome, Edge, Electron apps) are current before Tuesday.

**Sources:** [Google Cloud / GTIG](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/armory-castle-exploits-vsphere), [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/virtualization/2026/11/23/google-claims-last-weeks-critical-vmware-bug-was-under-attack-in-september/5311547), [CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)

### 1.2 GL.iNet GoodCloud flaw exploited to implant malware on routers and KVM devices (NEW)
On **20 Nov**, GL.iNet confirmed that an attacker exploited a flaw in its **GoodCloud** cloud remote-management service to access customer devices and install malware on some routers and **KVM devices**. GL.iNet says it has fixed the cloud flaw and blocked the access path, so devices cannot be newly infected that way. Those fixes do **not** remove malware from devices that are already infected. Users report backdoors and reverse shells.

The indicator GL.iNet has published is a process running as **`crond -i /tmp/.log`**. Forum members warn that this check misses some infections. GL.iNet is still working out whether **computers connected to infected KVMs** were compromised. It has not disclosed the root cause, the number of affected devices or whether data was taken. A community analysis says the GoodCloud client passes an unchecked cloud-message value to the shell, but nobody has confirmed that this is the exploited flaw.

Fixed versions:
- **KVM:** firmware **1.10.3+** for the RM10, RM1, RM1V2, RM1PE and RM4PE; **1.10.3 beta+** for the RM10RC; **1.8.7+** for the RMQ1.
- **Routers:** stable security firmware was scheduled for **22 Nov**.

**Action:** Inventory GL.iNet devices, especially KVMs in IT and OT admin paths and travel routers used by staff. Disable GoodCloud where it is not needed. Check each device for the `crond -i /tmp/.log` process, unknown listeners and outbound shells. Where in doubt, reset to factory settings, then flash the fixed firmware rather than just upgrading in place. Treat hosts behind an infected KVM as suspect: review their logins and EDR telemetry from **20 Nov** onward and rotate credentials typed through the KVM.

**Sources:** [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackers-exploit-glinet-cloud-flaw-to-infect-routers-kvm-devices/), [Daily CyberSecurity](https://securityonline.info/gl-inet-cloud-zero-day-exploited/)

### 1.3 NGINX2Root on PAN-OS: remaining hotfixes due today (follow-up, ONGOING)
Palo Alto Networks' portfolio advisory for **CVE-2026-123633** and **CVE-2026-123007** (CVSS 4.0 **9.2**, unauthenticated) still lists three PAN-OS hotfixes with **ETA 23 Nov**:
- **12.1.7-h15** (affected: 12.1.5 up to 12.1.7-h14)
- **11.2.10-h19** and **11.2.13-h6** (affected: the 11.2.8 – 11.2.16-h2 range)

Builds already available: **12.2.4-h3**, **12.1.10-h6** and **11.2.16-h3**. Only PAN-OS and Panorama with **HTTP/2 enabled** are exposed. Palo Alto reports no exploitation, and neither CVE is in KEV. Rapid7's PoC for CVE-2026-123007 is public, and fake exploit repositories are circulating.
**Action:** Install today's hotfixes as soon as they are published, or move to a fixed branch. Until then, keep HTTP/2 disabled. On standalone NGINX, run **1.31.9 / 1.30.7** or later. Keep checking appliance vendors' portfolio advisories: Splunk, for example, has already published one for NGINX.
**Sources:** [Palo Alto Networks advisory](https://security.paloaltonetworks.com/CVE-2026-123633), [Splunk advisories](https://advisory.splunk.com/)

### 1.4 Cleverbridge breach: card numbers, expiry dates and CVVs copied (NEW)
**Cleverbridge** runs checkout, payments and subscriptions for software and SaaS vendors. Its published customers include Red Hat, SUSE, Tenable, Veeam, Jamf, Sony and Parallels. It detected unauthorized activity on **4 Nov**, took systems offline and restored them the same day. Its investigation later found that a third party had **copied stored payment card data**. Notifications began on **20 Nov**.

A notice seen by BleepingComputer says the stolen data includes the **card number, expiry date and CVV/CVC**, but no name or billing details. Cleverbridge has not said how many people were affected, which merchants' customers they bought from, how the attackers got in or how long they had access. It says the attack was not ransomware. PCI DSS forbids storing CVVs after authorization, so it is unclear why the codes were available to steal.
**Action:**
- **Card issuers and fraud teams:** expect card-not-present fraud on cards used at Cleverbridge-hosted software stores.
- **Corporate purchasing:** check which corporate cards bought software through Cleverbridge checkouts, and ask the issuer to reissue them.
- **Vendors that sell through Cleverbridge:** prepare customer communications and expect phishing that impersonates your renewal notices.

**Sources:** [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/cleverbridge-warns-hackers-stole-credit-card-numbers-cvvs-in-data-breach/), [Cyber Security News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/cleverbridge-data-breach/)

- **Mongoose embedded networking library 7.24:** Cesanta reportedly fixed **seven** flaws. The worst is a ZDI-reported heap overflow (CVSS **9.8**) in `read_conn()` of the built-in **MIP** TCP/IP stack: a TCP FIN packet that carries data skips a buffer resize. It is unauthenticated and fires **before any protocol parsing**, so an open TCP port is enough. Other fixes cover the TLS stack and the WebSocket parser. Some advisories include PoC material, and no exploitation is reported. Mongoose is built into firmware on many IoT and ICS devices, so fixes depend on device makers shipping rebuilt firmware. This rests on a single report: verify against Cesanta's advisories, and ask embedded suppliers whether they use Mongoose ≤ **7.23**. [Daily CyberSecurity](https://securityonline.info/mongoose-heap-overflow-rce-7-24/)

## 4. Threat Watch
- **ARMORY CASTLE (ex-UNC5221), PRC-nexus:** GTIG's write-up shows a campaign that used **two zero-days**: BeyondTrust Remote Support **CVE-2026-102764**, then vCenter **CVE-2026-123890**. Across the campaign it used three sets of custom malware: **BONESAW** and **SAWSNAKE** for vCenter and SAML, the **SLIMSAVAGE** and **CAGEMATCH** webshells, and the **BEARBONES** backdoor. The playbook is to get in through an edge appliance, pivot across Linux appliances, take over vCenter (SSO admin, forged SAML) and steal data from ESXi as root. It resembles the BRICKSTORM-era UNC5221 tradecraft. Google has not said how many victims there are. Defenders should treat vCenter and ESXi logs as primary hunt sources, because EDR coverage on these appliances is usually absent. [Google Cloud / GTIG](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/armory-castle-exploits-vsphere)
- **RansomwareHelp CTO indicted (follow-up, named):** KrebsOnSecurity names the defendant indicted in Rhode Island as **Juan Ricardo Palacio Escobar**, co-founder of **ransomwarehelp.com**. An FBI sting, with agents posing as both victim and ransomware gang, allegedly showed the firm paid attackers for decryptors while telling clients it used "proprietary cryptographic tools". In the sting it paid about **$6,000** and billed **$16,010**. Victims who used "no-pay" recovery firms should check whether a ransom was paid on their behalf, which matters for sanctions and reporting. [KrebsOnSecurity](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2026/11/cto-of-ransomwarehelp-indicted-for-wire-fraud/)
- **Com-affiliated vishing (Unit 42, context):** Unit 42's 20 Nov report covers the voice-phishing data-theft waves since August against finance, healthcare and legal targets in the US, UK and Australia. Named actors and clusters include:
  - **Bling Libra** (ShinyHunters)
  - **Redact**, **Pink**, **Falcon** and **Helix**
  - the new **Vortex** (CL-CRI-1264) and **Cinder** (CL-CRI-1274) clusters, which overlap with **UNC6671**

  Before Thanksgiving-week staffing gaps, harden help-desk identity checks and SaaS OAuth app approvals. [Unit 42](https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/com-affiliate-vishing-attacks/)
